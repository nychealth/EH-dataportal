Susceptibility to harm

The FVI combines the risk of physical exposure to different flooding types with socioeconomic factors that increase the risk of harm and affect how difficult it may be to prepare, evacuate or recover. These include factors like the proportion of people in a neighborhood who:

  • Are older or younger, have chronic illnesses, or have disabilities or mobility impairments.
  • May face disruption to food, income, or healthcare access - including people who are isolated or have language barriers, people who are socially or economically marginalized, or people with unstable living situations (including people living in institutional settings or who are incarcerated).
  • Own their home, which can increase cost and time it takes to recover.

About the data

Each layer of the FVI is the result of the geometric average of the FSHRI combined with different physical exposure indices. Remember, a neighborhood with low risk for one type of flooding may still have high vulnerability to another type of flooding. The Flood Vulnerability Index (FVI) is part of the Climate Vulnerability, Impact, and Adaptation Analysis (VIA) report from the Mayor's Office of Climate and Environmental Justice.
