Climate and Health in

South Beach - Tottenville
Climate and Health

As the climate changes, we track New York City’s risk for extreme heat, storms and flooding, and episodes of elevated ozone. These have important public health impacts: already, heat kills more people than all other natural disasters.

Climate hazards

Some neighborhoods are hotter than others, which can make it costly or difficult to stay healthy for those residents

Surface temperatures vary based on vegetative cover (which promotes cooling), as well as by materials that retain heat (like paved roads, sidewalks, and buildings). Hotter neighborhoods tend to have more heat-exacerbated deaths associated with extreme heat events.

What is the City doing?

Plants trees, funds Cool Roof projects, and supports heat awareness trainings and more to address extreme heat.
Set a goal to achieve carbon neutrality by 2050 using the OneNYC strategic plan.
Helps low-income households buy and install air conditioners.
Operates the largest electric municipal fleet in the nation.
Supports energy-efficiency changes in municipal and community buildings.

What you can do

Be a Buddy and check in on neighbors to help them stay cool when it’s hot.
Get info during weather emergencies.
Request a street tree to help cool your neighborhood.
