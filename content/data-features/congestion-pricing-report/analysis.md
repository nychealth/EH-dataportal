This study is designed to isolate the effect of congestion pricing on air quality. 

Air quality changes, so we expect to see a difference before congestion pricing, and after congestion pricing. Instead of studying the difference before and after congestion pricing, we study the difference between the air quality we observed after congestion pricing, and what the air quality would have been if there were no congestion pricing. 

This approach uses data from a control site on the Van Wyck Expressway, a place that’s not affected by congestion pricing. We use before-and-after data from the control site to model what the air quality would be elsewhere *if there was no congestion pricing*. 

This gives us <span style="color:darkorange;font-weight:bold">hypothetical</span> “business as usual” data. We then use statistics to compare this to <span style="color:blue;font-weight:bold">observed</span> air quality data. If the difference between hypothetical and observed is statistically different than zero, then congestion pricing caused the difference. 

This is called an interrupted time series study with control. To date, **this is the most comprehensive evaluation of congestion pricing’s impact on air quality.**
