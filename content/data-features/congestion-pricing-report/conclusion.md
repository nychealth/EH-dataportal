This evaluation focuses on whether Congestion Relief Tolling affected air pollution levels in neighborhoods that have been monitored for years through the New York City Community Air Survey. Additional monitoring supported by the MTA allowed us to determine whether levels of PM2.5, NO2, NO and BC were different than they would have been if tolling had never been started.

We found that at EJ neighborhood sites - where there were concerns of increased traffic and its effect on air quality - air pollution levels remained the same or went down slightly, although most likely not because of Congestion Relief Tolling.

Within the CRZ, neighborhood pollution levels either remained the same or went down slightly. These were also not due to the tolling program, but more likely, due to citywide trends over time.

This is not an unexpected finding: traffic makes up only about 15% of PM2.5 emissions, for example, so a roughly 10% reduction in traffic volume in the CRZ may not substantially change air pollution compared to other sources of emissions.

These findings, along with the improvements to the transit system and mitigation measures, emphasize the benefits that the tolling program is bringing to New York City as a whole.
