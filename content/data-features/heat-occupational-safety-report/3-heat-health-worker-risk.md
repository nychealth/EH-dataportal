A common misconception is that heat causes only traditional heat-related illnesses, such as heat exhaustion, heat stroke, and dehydration. While these conditions increase sharply on hot days, heat is associated with a much broader range of workplace injuries and illnesses.

On a 95°F day, heat illness and other medical conditions, including cardiovascular events such as heart attacks, increase by 44%. But we also see a 23% increase in mental health conditions and a 25% increase in motor vehicle incidents, along with increases in other traumatic injuries, which are much more common. This range reflects the many physiological, cognitive, and social pathways through which heat can affect workers. Heat strain can impair attention, judgment, coordination, and reaction time, while poor sleep can affect mental health and social interactions, creating additional risks at work.
